# Research Audit & Finalization

Review the research brief above as a **critical research advisor**, not as someone who should simply agree with the proposed idea.

Your job is to help us move from a broad idea to a **clear, evidence-backed and defensible project problem**.

## 1. Audit the research

* Verify the important claims in the brief using current, credible sources.
* Find Kathmandu-specific academic papers, government reports, transport master plans, existing systems and related projects that are missing.
* Identify claims that are weak, outdated, unsupported, or misleading.
* Distinguish clearly between **what is established evidence, what is our hypothesis, and what is speculation**.
* Do not assume the proposed research gap is actually a gap.

## 2. Audit existing solutions

Map what has already been proposed or implemented for Kathmandu, including:

* traffic management
* route rationalization
* public-transport optimization
* vehicle/fleet allocation
* journey planning
* intelligent transport systems
* traffic prediction
* demand management
* multimodal transport
* traffic assignment / network optimization
* relevant government and private-sector systems

For each, explain:

**Problem → Existing approach → Result/status → Limitation → Remaining gap**

Pay particular attention to solutions that are already very similar to our proposed demand-distribution idea.

## 3. Challenge our core idea

Critically test the assumption that:

> Kathmandu's congestion can meaningfully be reduced by distributing travel demand across alternative routes/options rather than simply optimizing each individual's fastest route.

Tell us:

* Is this actually a significant Kathmandu problem?
* Is it already being solved?
* Is it technically/research-wise meaningful?
* Is there enough room for a genuinely new contribution?
* Are we misunderstanding the root cause?
* Is there a better problem hiding underneath our original idea?

If the idea is weak, **say so and propose better alternatives**.

## 4. Find and rank possible research gaps

Generate 3–5 candidate problem directions based on the evidence.

Rank each by:

* Kathmandu relevance
* real-world importance
* research novelty
* existing literature
* feasibility for a student project
* measurable impact
* potential for a meaningful engineering contribution

Do not choose based on what sounds technologically impressive.

## 5. Finalize the problem

After the audit, recommend **one strongest problem direction**.

Define:

* Final problem statement
* Why the problem matters
* Existing solutions
* Why existing solutions are insufficient
* Research gap
* Proposed contribution
* Primary research question
* Secondary research questions
* Hypothesis, if appropriate
* What success would mean
* What should be explicitly out of scope

Make the final definition precise enough that we could later design the actual system around it.

## 6. Do NOT decide implementation yet

Do **not** spend time choosing:

* programming languages
* frameworks
* databases
* cloud
* microservices
* ML models
* architecture
* UI
* hardware

Those decisions come only after the problem and research contribution are finalized.

## 7. Be brutally critical

Do not preserve our original idea just because we proposed it.

If research shows that:

> "This already exists."

say it.

If:

> "This is not actually the main problem."

say it.

If:

> "The proposed novelty is too weak."

say it.

If a different problem is significantly better, recommend it.

The goal is **not to validate our idea**.

The goal is to find the **best real problem and most defensible contribution we can pursue for Kathmandu**.

## Final output

End with a concise **Research Decision Report** containing:

1. What we originally thought the problem was
2. What the evidence says the problem actually is
3. Existing solutions
4. What remains unsolved
5. Recommended research gap
6. Final problem statement
7. Final research questions
8. Proposed contribution
9. Why this is worth building
10. Key risks/unknowns that still need investigation
11. Recommended next research step

Cite sources throughout and prioritize primary/official sources and peer-reviewed research wherever possible.
